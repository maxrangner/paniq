# Game vision

## Decided: core premise

Paniq is a darkly comedic, real-time crowd-survival game inspired by the
indirect-control lineage of *Lemmings*. The player influences an autonomous
crowd during a changing disaster and tries to save as many entities as possible.
The primary result is the percentage of the crowd saved.

The player never selects an entity and orders it to walk or perform a task.
They instead change the environment and, in later designs, may temporarily
change what an entity is capable of doing. The exact form of those interventions
is not decided.

Death is a central failure outcome. It is presented as exaggerated, dark comic
consequence rather than as realistic disaster drama.

## Decided: player experience

Paniq should create fun, chaos, surprise, and the satisfaction of improving a
rescue. Hazards evolve and people affect one another: panic may spread,
information may travel, and individual choices may create chain reactions.

This is **bounded, explainable chaos**. A run can produce an unexpected result,
but the player must be able to observe the causes, form a better plan, and
improve through replay. Randomness must not hide why an intervention succeeded
or failed.

## Design pillars

1. **Indirect control:** influence situations and capabilities, never issue
   direct orders to individual entities.
2. **Autonomous crowd:** the crowd reacts to hazards, the environment, and each
   other; it is not a collection of identical particles.
3. **Readable emergence:** simple, visible rules produce surprising chains with
   understandable causes.
4. **Dynamic survival:** disasters and crowd conditions change during real-time
   play, creating urgency without removing the player's ability to respond.
5. **Dark comic consequence:** saving entities matters; disastrous failures are
   explicit, exaggerated, and part of the game's black-comedy appeal.

## Deliberately open

- The player's in-world identity.
- Specific disasters and locations.
- Exact environmental actions, Influence economy, cards, slow motion, and role
  assignment.
- Crowd size, individual traits, relationships, and which information is hidden.
- Campaign, sandbox, and other long-term content structure.

Locations and situations will vary; no single narrative setting is committed.

## Not in the foundation

Networking, open-world simulation, procedural cities, monetization, purchased
assets, and mobile deployment are not foundation requirements.
