# Codebase audit, 2026-10-02

A full read of the runtime (58,000 lines of plain C# that did not exist three
weeks ago), the tests, the tools and the live documents, measured against the
three requirements for the finished game (large crowds, feelings not events,
handcrafted levels possibly on several storeys), the simulation rules, and the
20 ms tick budget at 200–500 people. Nothing was changed; this page is the
list the refactor stones follow. Items are grouped by theme and ranked inside
each theme. Every `file:line` is under `Assets/Paniq/Runtime/Simulation/`
unless it says otherwise.

Two facts frame everything below. First, the bones are right: one owner of the
tick, integer-only geometry, a seeded generator, stable ids everywhere, the
`IThreat` seam, a spatial index behind every "who is near me", and a display
that only ever reads snapshots. None of the auditors found a replay-breaking
nondeterminism or a behaviour that switches on an event's name. Second, the
code grew faster than its structure: the same five little routines ("walk to a
door and get through it", "fetch a thing", "give up") exist seven to twelve
times, four files are over 2,000 lines, and a third of the per-tick cost is
work that a short list or a cached table would make free.

## A. Where the simulation's time goes (and the five levers)

Last recorded: ~12–15 ms a tick at 500 panicking people in the editor, about
4.7 of it inside `PhysicsWorld.Step`. The remaining 7–8 ms, from reading:

1. **Critical — frightened people re-ask every option every tick, and the
   options do pathfinding inside.** `PanicBehaviour.cs:263-273` runs every
   `IPanicOption.Decide` for every scared person every tick (not only at
   `NextPanicDecisionTick`). Inside them: `ExtinguisherBehaviour.cs:238`
   builds a flow field (`NearestBurningPerson`, :632-669) *before* the trait
   gate at :239, so every fleeing coward pays for a field; `AlarmBehaviour.cs:116-122`
   a `ReachFrom` per tick per candidate; `HelpBehaviour.cs:136-160` a gather
   plus a threat distance per candidate; `KeycardSystem.cs:551` a
   `TryFindRoute` per tick; `HandGatherBehaviour.cs:100` → `HandHeaveBehaviour.NearestCrate`
   (:120-155) over all objects. `Navigation.cs:45` caps field builds at 8 a
   tick, so **the first eight or so scared people (by index) get real routes
   and everybody after them steers blind on the straight-line fallback** —
   low-numbered people are systematically smarter, and the 32-field cache
   (:27) thrashes against ~300 distinct 2 m reach keys at 500 people.
   *Refactor:* options are asked only at decision ticks or on `ThinkAgainSoon`;
   one per-agent per-tick perception scratch (room, in danger, nearest threat
   point, felt hand, reach field if budget allows) computed once and handed to
   every option; trait gates before any spatial query, everywhere.

2. **Critical — room-to-room routes are recomputed per person per door.**
   `WorldGeometry.FindRoute` (:774-864) is a Dijkstra per call. Callers:
   `DoorBehaviour.ChooseExitDoor` :181-184 per outside door; `ChooseRefugeDoor`
   :687-690 per room plus `IsRoomFull` (:727-751, a crowd gather per room);
   `CoolRefugeIsReachable` :490-501 calls `ChooseRefugeDoor` again for a
   yes/no; `HasAnotherWayOut` :1718-1748 (doors × route) reached **every
   tick** from `DoorTarget` (:908) via `PanicBehaviour.cs:410`;
   `AnnounceWaysOut` :92-136 per door opened × every agent × route;
   `DirectorSystem.TakeTheReading` :865-944 `CanReachAWayOut` per agent in two
   passes; `ErrandBehaviour.ChooseWayOut` :816-855. And the door-walk fields
   `FindRoute` leans on (`WorldGeometry.WalkTo` :949-973) have **no per-tick
   budget** and are all thrown away by `TheBuildingChangedShape` (:1394,
   plus `navigation.Forget()`), so the first routes after a crate settles can
   trigger dozens of full-grid builds in one tick. This is the mechanism behind
   the note "route queries flood the whole building".
   *Refactor:* an all-pairs room table (`nextDoor[from][to]`, `cost`)
   rebuilt only when a door's passability version changes; per-room "nearest
   way out and first door toward it" once per tick; door walks under the same
   build budget with the straight-line leg as the fallback; fields stamped
   with the patch they were built against so a crate settling in the stockroom
   does not forget the corridor.

3. **Critical — a crate settling or a desk shoved 15 cm re-marks every doorway
   in the building with an unbucketed search.** `NavigationGrid.Rebuild`
   (:315-342) patches only the changed cells but then calls `MarkDoorways`
   (:235-267), which re-marks every doorway (≈225 cells each) with
   `NearestEdge` (:283-296), a linear scan over every wall piece and table
   edge with up to three square roots each: ~3.4 million segment distances per
   rebuild on 30 rooms, several times that on 50. The bucketed version already
   exists (`MeasureClearance` :413-477) and is not used here. `Rebuild` also
   calls `BuildWalls`/`BuildDoorways`, which allocate. Triggered by
   `WorldGeometry.MoveTable` :1354-1381, `SettleHeavyThing`/`LiftHeavyThing`
   :1467-1502 (every box of a fallen tower, twice), `TryPlaceHole`, and
   **at level load** by `SetHeavyThings` :1446-1460 (a full-grid unbucketed
   rebuild right after the constructor measured the same grid properly).
   Door walks also allocate a full-grid `int[]` per door side after every
   invalidation (:969) — ~25 MB of garbage per crate on a 50-room floor.
   *Refactor:* the segment bucket grid becomes a persistent member, patched
   when walls or tables change; re-mark only doorways whose reach touches the
   patch; heavy things go into the constructor; keep the walk arrays and flag
   them invalid instead of nulling them; `FlowField.Build` (:115-121) uses a
   generation stamp instead of clearing four full-grid arrays per build.

4. **High — about fifteen passes over the whole crowd every tick, each for a
   handful of people.** `BurningPeopleThreat.Advance` :60-71;
   `InfluenceSystem.Advance` :757; `Run.ResolveCurrentContact` :1109;
   `LeaderBehaviour.CountFollowers` :358-374 (two passes, for a
   presentation-only number); `ExtinguisherBehaviour.Spray` :492-506;
   `HelpBehaviour.PullDragged` :431; `ItemBehaviour.FollowCarriers` :344;
   `keycards.FollowHolders`; `DoorBehaviour.CarryTheFallenThroughDoorways`
   :1931; `ResolveRoomChangesAndEscapes` :2254; `Run.SettleThePurseAndTheHand`
   :1037-1068 (counting outcomes for a purse the level has switched off);
   `RoundSystem` :201-296 (three passes); `Run.CountClearOfFire` :1147 (for a
   snapshot field nobody reads); `GroupSystem.PullToward` :146-167 (O(N²) for
   stick-together) and `AnchorOf` :92-111. `BurningBehaviour`'s alight buffer
   and `HelpBehaviour.helpedBy` already show the right pattern.
   *Refactor:* `Crowd` keeps short index lists — Holding, Spraying, Dragging,
   Following, Burning, Down, group members — maintained where the state
   changes (one place each); counts kept on the transitions.

5. **High — the same question answered many times per person per tick.**
   Room lookups: `WorldGeometry.RoomAt` :426-449, `RoomOf` :485-495,
   `RoomStoodIn` :510-530, `RoomAtPoint` :532-549 each trust the grid cell
   only when the footprint is wholly inside a room and otherwise **scan every
   room** — i.e. in doorways and against walls, exactly where a crowd packs;
   ~80 call sites, ~10 per scared person per tick. The fix ("give each cell the
   short list of rooms that touch it") was named on 2026-09-21 and never done.
   The hand: `InfluenceSystem.FeltBy` recomputed five to ten times per agent
   per tick (`Advance` :773-777, then `TryGetPull/TryGetLivePull/TryGetLivePush`
   from CalmBehaviour, HandGather, HandHeave, DoorBehaviour per door scored,
   TellSystem, FearSystem, Extinguisher, Keycard, Alarm); `WalkTo` :924-957
   loops all doors. Perception: `PerceptionSystem.cs:128` gathers an 8 m
   circle per calm person per tick looking for bolters (few) — invert it.
   Threats: `BurningThingsThreat` :58-86, :91-109 loop every burning thing with
   a `RoomAtPoint` inside each query while `Advance` :51-53 is empty (the
   people threat caches correctly). Walls: `AddWallRepulsion` :1885-1899 loops
   all tables per moving person (500 × 50 square roots a tick) and the four
   wall pushes :1906-1925 assume a rectangle; `TableAt` :1560, `RouteCrossesTable`
   :1576, `PushOutOfTables` :1600, `ObstacleAt` :1505 likewise.
   *Refactor:* per cell: the room (doorway cells marked "between A and B"
   rather than assigned to one), clearance, and a packed direction to the
   nearest solid — then room lookup, wall repulsion and "am I in a doorway" are
   one cell read each and work for any room shape. `Advance` writes
   `hand.FeltThisTick` once. Each threat caches its per-tick set; `Threats`
   keeps a compact active list and a per-room presence summary.

6. **High — the engine readback is ~15–20 native calls per body per tick.**
   `PhysicsWorld.Step` :1343-1389 and `ReadBack` :1470-1517: per body,
   `isKinematic` three times, `IsSleeping()` twice, `AddForce` for gravity,
   `worldCenterOfMass`, and `bounds` per collider (PhysX computes an AABB).
   At 500 people and 1,000 things that is 25–30k managed→native transitions
   before any simulation code runs — plausibly 1.5–2.5 ms of the "physics"
   number, which wraps all of this, not just the solver. `PeopleBodies.Drive`
   :399-410 writes `rotation`, `sharedMaterial` (through a tuple-keyed
   dictionary) and `AddForce`+`WakeUp` for every standing person every tick
   whether anything changed or not. Contacts are walked three times a tick
   (`PhysicsObjectSystem.AfterStep` :2153, `CollisionSystem.Resolve` :41,
   `PeopleBodies.FeelTheSqueeze` :648).
   *Refactor:* cache kinematic in `Body`; compute centre and bottom from the
   known parts; `useGravity` with `Physics.gravity`; read poses in one
   `TransformAccessArray` pass; write heading/grip only on change; one contact
   pass. Measure first: split `Step()` with four stopwatches (pre-step,
   `Simulate`, `ReadBack`, `MergeIncoming`); the stress profile never exercises
   the rebuild paths (its boxes are 3 kg, under `OnTheMapFromGrams`), so add a
   fallen tower and a 50-room floor to it.

7. **Medium — one person is ~21 heap objects.** `Agent.cs:68-90`: 19
   sub-state class instances plus five arrays per agent; `Run.cs:896-971`
   touches seven of them per person per tick. Within plain C#: hot sub-states
   (`Body`, `Fear`, `Intent`, `Burning`, `Tug`) as structs inlined or as
   parallel struct arrays on `Crowd`; cold ones stay. Iteration order is
   untouched, so fingerprints survive.

8. **Medium — allocation and small waste.** `FearSystem.Staggered` :108-119
   and `CueSystem.Staggered` :109-120: a closure and a `HashSet.RemoveWhere`
   per startle, and O(n²) probing when a bell reaches 500 people (ring bitset).
   Snapshot: `AppendOnlyView` is a class allocated per fill (`CausalEventLog.cs:113`,
   `FireSystem.cs:641`); `power.Sparks()` allocates a list; `Run.GetTable`
   :566 allocates an array; `GetPhysicsObject`+`WithBurn` build each thing's
   struct twice. `RunSnapshot` count properties :835-975 are O(n) scans read
   per frame by the HUD (`PrototypeHud.cs:111`, five scans for one line).
   `LeaderBehaviour.cs:140, :216` delegate allocations; `DirectorSystem.cs:708`
   a list per push; `ErrandBehaviour.cs:292, :657` concatenate an enum into a
   debug string on simulation state. `Run.Step` :904-933 fans out nine hooks
   per person (`KeycardSystem.Notice` for all 500 whenever a card exists;
   `SwipeIfInReach` walks every door per scared holder).

## B. Where the display's time goes (one lever)

The boundary is clean: every write reaches the run through
`RunDriver.Queue*` → `Run.QueueCommand`; no Unity `Random`/`Time` in the
simulation; the hands-off round has its own `Run`, cloned data and physics
scene. The problem is volume.

1. **Critical — 47 GameObjects and ~37 renderer components per person.**
   `Presentation/AgentViews.cs:69-128`, `AgentIconViews.cs:64-162`,
   `Effects/FlameEmitter.cs:24-42`: capsule, band, two eyes, two pupils, glow
   cube, a vision-cone `LineRenderer`, and an icon rig of 39 objects (strokes,
   arcs, snowflake, stars, four `TextMesh`es, star, hand, keycard). 23,500
   objects at 500 people; ~25 engine calls per person per frame; vision cones
   **on by default** (seven `SetPosition` and a mesh rebuild each); 500 legacy
   `TextMesh` numbers always active; 2,000 eye sphere draws.
   *Refactor:* `AgentViews` becomes a crowd renderer with zero objects per
   person, exactly as `FireView` already draws flames: per-person maths into
   `Matrix4x4[]`, `Graphics.RenderMeshInstanced` bucketed by colour, eyes as
   two instanced batches, cones as one dynamic mesh, icons and numbers from one
   glyph atlas as instanced quads, the see-through silhouette as one more
   pass. Keep screen positions per person for the end-screen click.

2. **High — every coloured thing is an individual draw.** `PresentationMaterials.cs:116-129`,
   `BoxViews.cs:401-416, 559`, `RoomView.cs:626-631, 757-761`, `AgentViews.cs:607, 618`:
   per-renderer `MaterialPropertyBlock`s with `SetColor` every frame
   unconditionally. URP's SRP Batcher does not batch renderers carrying a
   property block, so bodies, props, tables and doors are each set up
   separately. Instancing for people (B1); for props, bucketed instancing or at
   least colour-on-change.

3. **Medium — per-frame habits.** IMGUI builds fresh strings two to three
   passes per frame (`PrototypeHud.cs:110-112, 206-243, 418-448` — a
   `string[10]` per person for the stats table, `HasTheKeycard` scanning props
   per row; `RoundScreens.cs:154` + `RunDriver.cs:182-205` a list per pass;
   `DebugView.cs:119-143`). `foreach` over `IReadOnlyList` boxes an enumerator
   (`RoomView.cs:420, 604, 619, 663-678`, `RunPresentation.cs:439`,
   `SprayView.cs:28`); each swing door walks **all agents** per frame to see
   who is in the gap. `EventSigns.cs:89, 108` copies every event of the round
   into a dictionary though ids are dense (`Events[id-1]`). `PlayerInput.cs:273-275,
   322, 436-465`: `Physics.SyncTransforms` every frame, a raycast, and every
   prop projected to screen every frame for a hover line. A glow cube per
   person, prop and table at build time; `Emit(1)` per particle.
   *Refactor:* UI Toolkit with labels updated on change and a virtualised
   list; index loops; `DoorSnapshot` carries "somebody in the doorway"; one
   `Picking` service projecting people and things once per frame into a
   screen grid (removes the door/alarm colliders and `SyncTransforms`); one
   `Strokes` factory and one `Easing` set (duplicates: `EaseOutBack` ×2,
   `SetColor/WithAlpha` ×2, `CreateLit` ×2, font load ×2, `LineRenderer`
   construction ×6).

4. **Never measured.** `StressProfiler` builds a `Run`, never the display.
   Extend it to instantiate `RunPresentation` and record frame time; in the
   editor, `Profiler.BeginSample` per view in `RunPresentation.Update:255-272`,
   Frame Debugger batch counts, GC per frame, and the Tab panel as the A/B
   (cones, numbers, marks off).

## C. Dead ends against the three requirements

1. **Storeys: nothing can hold a second floor, and the newest code assumes
   one.** `SimulationPrimitives.cs:7` `LogicalPosition` is (x, z);
   `ScenarioData.cs:783` `RoomDefinition` has no storey; `ValidateRooms`
   :1782-1789 *rejects* two rooms whose rectangles overlap, which is what two
   storeys are; a door names one room (:306-316) and the far room is inferred
   by a flush-wall test (`ValidateDoorNeighbour` :1885-1933), so a stair cannot
   be written down. `PhysicsWorld.cs:340-359` one floor at y = 0 and one
   ceiling at wall height; absolute heights in `IsBuildingBetween` :970,
   `IsClearToStand` :906, `LayDown` :754, `AddPerson` :628, `AddTable` :420.
   `NavigationGrid`, `FlowField`, `UniformGridIndex`, `Crowd`, the fire grid
   and `WorldGeometry.FireArea` :413 are each one 2-D layer. In presentation,
   `PresentationUtility.cs:62-65` returns y = 0 and ~20 files add their own
   `Vector3.up * k`; `CameraRig` frames XZ only; `BuildingShellView` is one
   slab. The decision of 2026-09-24 ("prepared for now") has not been acted
   on, and the cost rises with every file.
   *Refactor:* `Storey` on `RoomDefinition`; a `Place` (position + storey) at
   the room/door/grid boundary; grids as arrays indexed by storey (same code);
   storey base height applied in one place in `PhysicsWorld` with a floor and
   ceiling slab per storey; `DoorDefinition.BeyondRoomId` (nullable) and
   `DoorKind { Door, Opening, Swing, Stair }` so the neighbour is data for a
   stair and derived otherwise; `WorldGeometry.RoomBeyond` the one answer to
   "other side"; one `ScenePlacement.Of(position, storey)` in presentation.

2. **Rooms are axis-aligned rectangles addressed by wall side.** 68 uses of
   `WallSide` and seven switch-on-side blocks in `WorldGeometry` (:351, :384,
   :1129, :1218, :1282, :1655, :1692, :1775, :1937); a door is (room, side,
   offset, width) (`ScenarioData.cs:345-347`); `CanSeeBetween` :610-674
   decides sight by room adjacency and door gaps, not wall segments;
   `TryPlaceHole` finds walls by room side. Yet both consumers of walls —
   `PhysicsWorld.SetWalls` :365-398 and `NavigationGrid.Wall` — already take
   segments with thickness, and the grid already knows each cell's room.
   *Refactor (later, when a plan needs it, but shaped for now):* the floor
   plan as wall segments plus room polygons rasterised once into `cellRoom`;
   a door as (segment, offset); sight by walking the ray through the segment
   buckets from A3; a blast hole as "cut this segment here". A tipped table's
   footprint is today the axis-aligned box around the rotated block
   (`PhysicsWorld.cs:466-488`) — a phantom much larger than the desk.

3. **The fire is baked into the public surface, undoing the `IThreat` seam
   at the display.** `Run.cs:19` `FireHazardIdValue`; :483-491
   `FireActive/FireCellCount/FireGrid*/FireOrigin`; :1145 `CountClearOfFire`;
   `RunSnapshot.cs:710-716` `Fill(fireActive, fireOrigin, fireCells…)`;
   `ScenarioData.cs:1328` seeds the id set with the fire's id. A flood means a
   second set of snapshot fields and a parallel drawing path.
   *Refactor:* `IThreat.FillSnapshot(buffer)`; `RunSnapshot.Threats` as a
   list of `ThreatSnapshot { Kind, RootEventId, cells/points }`; presentation
   picks a drawer by kind. Fighting is likewise hard-wired to fire
   (`ExtinguisherBehaviour.cs:124-134, 208, 221, 253, 310-321`,
   `LeaderBehaviour.cs:177` read `fire.*`/`flammables.*` by name): a small
   `ICounterable` on a threat (nearest fightable point, small enough to fight,
   which tool counters it) makes sandbags against a flood content, not code.

4. **The Director is one level's script in code.** `DirectorSystem.cs:109-136`
   (`Rung { Bin, Socket, FuseBox }`), :386-474 (`Climb`), :998-1049 (`Push`)
   reach into `fire`, `flammables`, `power`, `traps` by name; `DirectorSettings.FirstIncidentThings`
   defaults to `PrototypeBuilding.MeetingRoomBins()` (`ScenarioSettings.cs:2829`)
   — a settings class defaulting to one office's bins. *Refactor:* the ladder
   as scenario data (an ordered list of `Incident { trigger, target id, delay
   range, settles }` over a generic `IIncident.Start/IsOut/IsLoose`); the cap
   reads "on course" from the per-room way-out table (A2).

5. **`ScenarioSettings` (37 groups, 3,415 lines) and `ScenarioData` (2,324)
   are god objects, and `new ScenarioData()` *is* the office.**
   `ScenarioData.cs:1079-1119` lists 37 fields; :1194-1246 a 47-line
   hand-written `Clone`; :1248-1388 `Validate` whose null-check list
   (:1260-1266) **omits `Power`, `HandCharge` and `Tells`** yet calls their
   `Validate()` at :1295, :1300, :1305 — a missing group throws a
   `NullReferenceException` instead of the friendly message. :846-1076 is 230
   lines of version history in a code comment that the rules say lives in
   `docs/history/version-history.md`. Every content array defaults to
   `PrototypeBuilding.Default*()` (:1081, :1121-1163), so every other level is
   "the office minus things" and the subtraction already leaks:
   `TestBuildings.Blank` (:442-457, uncommitted) must know every field and
   misses `Director.FirstIncidentThings` and `BlastHoles`. Settings mix crowd
   rules, per-threat rules, level content and switches, and the player's hand.
   *Refactor:* four parts — `CrowdRules`, `HazardRules` (one block per
   `IThreat`, owned by it), `LevelContent` (rooms, doors, cast, props,
   tables, signs, cables, traps, timetable, cues, every `Enabled` switch;
   **default empty**, the office one builder among several), `HandRules` —
   each group implementing `ISettingsGroup { Clone(); Validate(); }` registered
   in one array so clone, validate and the null check are a loop.

6. **The bake pipeline is dead in practice, so "handcrafted levels" has no
   working path.** No scene in the repository contains a single
   `PaniqRoom/Door/Person` component (checked by script GUID); the office lives
   in `PrototypeBuilding.cs`, is copied into `FireReactionScenario.asset`, and
   `SimulationEditModeTests.cs:57-68` **requires the asset to equal the code
   defaults field by field**, so a successful bake (which also appends
   `+scene` to `ContentRevision`) fails the suite. `TestBuildings.Apply`
   (uncommitted) is a third building source that overwrites at runtime. The
   baker (`Editor/ScenarioBaker.cs:32, 46-106, 157-221, 384-397`) cannot
   author swing doors, openings, exit signs, cables, traps or a timetable;
   writes one hard-coded path; reorders rooms so the fire's room is `Rooms[0]`
   (room iteration order — and so replay — depends on where the designer drops
   the fire marker); and breaks door-ownership ties by object name. Every
   level reloads the same scene, whose name is spelled in five places
   (`LevelLoader.cs:50`, `Bootstrapper.cs:15`, `LevelDefinition.cs:20`,
   `CreateOfficeLevel.cs:27, 46`, `RoundScreens.cs:49`); `CreateOfficeLevel`
   duplicates values that also live in `TheOffice.asset` and field defaults.
   *Refactor:* follows C5 — `LevelDefinition` references a tuning asset and a
   building asset; `PrototypeBuilding` and `TestBuildings` become editor
   commands that *write* building assets (and a fixture builder for tests);
   the baker takes its target from the selected level; the fire room is found
   by id; one runner scene, levels are data, scene name in one place.

7. **Construction order is the replay.** `Run.cs:89-207` is a 110-line
   constructor whose `new` order fixes the random-draw order (comments at
   :97-100, :129-131, :141-143, :198-199); `Systems.cs:77-82` hand-lists 23
   bindables. Build systems with no draws, then one explicit `StartUp()` that
   performs the documented draw sequence; `IBindable` can go.

## D. How a person decides — the next stone, and why the duplication matters

Today there are two decision models. **Calm** chooses by one banded dice roll
(`CalmBehaviour.ChooseActivity`) and then runs *data* scripts
(`ErrandStep`/`CueDefinition`, interpreted by `ErrandBehaviour.BeginStep`).
**Frightened** chooses by a fixed priority list of code (`IPanicOption` chain:
Keycards ► HandHeave ► HandGather ► Leaders ► Extinguishers ► Help ► Alarm ►
Barricades, first match wins, asked every tick), with scoring only inside the
door choice. Interrupts are wired into both sides separately; a task begun on
one side is cut and re-answered on the other. The duplication that follows:

- "Walk to a door, open it, wait, give up" ×3: `ErrandBehaviour.cs:862-1230`,
  `DoorBehaviour.UpdateAttempt` :1047-1267, `FrightenedWalk.TryStep` :49-136 —
  three timeouts, three avoid-memory writes, two "shut it behind me" rules.
- "Go to a thing, pick it up, carry it, set it down" ×7: `ItemBehaviour`
  :166-297, `ExtinguisherBehaviour` :369-395, `KeycardSystem` :682-769,
  `BarricadeBehaviour` :274-351, `ErrandBehaviour.ClearTheDoor` :986-1094,
  `ErrandBehaviour.WedgeTheDoor` :1355-1446 (literally the barricade at a
  walk), `HandHeaveBehaviour` :246-343.
- "Give up and think again" ×12 with inconsistent side effects
  (`Influence.Done` vs `Interrupted` vs `GiveUp` for the same failure), so the
  hand behaves differently depending on *which* task failed.
- "What am I doing" lives in ~12 fields, and six hand-maintained lists
  classify activities (`Agent.IsOnAnErrand` :141-167, `ErrandBehaviour.IsInterruptible`
  :136-149, `NudgeSystem.IsLoitering` :201-214, `RoundSystem.IsBusy` :240-257,
  `ChairBehaviour.ResolveStanding` :666-679, `FearSystem.CalmDown` :621-626).
  Adding one activity touches all six.
- `PanicBehaviour.Decide` :150-448 mixes choosing with steering;
  `ConsiderClosingAgainstFire` :2219-2244 loops the room's doors every tick per
  scared person.
- "Stand in front of a door" spots ×5; `InfluenceSystem.places` is a
  `List<Place>(1)` shim from the multi-place design (:305-404).

**What one task model needs** (the planned next stone): one `Task` record on
the agent (kind, target by index, pace calm/frightened as a *parameter*,
patience drawn with `Jittered` at start, cause, offered-by, and a mood gate:
drop / suspend-and-resume / keep); one **offer queue**, which is
`CueSystem.Hand`/`PendingCue` (:422-441) generalised — every outside influence
(cue, leader's order, the hand, a noise, a nudge) becomes an offer with
`StartTick = Staggered(ReactionTick())`, taken up in the person's own turn; one
chooser run only at decision ticks, scoring candidate tasks with trait weights
× fear scaling (fear changes weights, never which code runs); a step library of
~8 primitives executed every tick, with `FrightenedWalk.TryStep` given a pace
and made everyone's walk, and one `EndTask(outcome)`; perception once per tick
and routes as tables (A1, A2) so the chooser can afford to score at 500. This
moves every fingerprint and lands as one stone; remove the pure-query random
draws (E4) first so the new draw count is readable.

## E. The owner's rules, where they are broken
